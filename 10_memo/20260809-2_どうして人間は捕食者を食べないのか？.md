**どうして人間は捕食者を食べないのか？**

Imagine / you are standing / in front of a grill. 想像してみてください。/ あなたが立っているのは / グリルの前です。

You have / a fresh cut of meat / right in front of you. 目の前には / 新鮮な肉の塊があります。

It looks perfect. 完璧に見えます。

It smells incredible. ものすごくいい匂いがします。

You've done this / a thousand times. これを / 何千回もやってきました。

You cook it. 肉を焼きます。

You sit down. 座ります。

You eat. 食べます。

Now, / here is a question / that will feel weirdly simple / at first. さて、/ ここで一つの質問です。/ 最初は妙に単純に感じるでしょう。

Why was that meat / from a cow / and not from a lion? なぜその肉は / 牛の肉だったのでしょう？/ ライオンではなく。

Not from a wolf? オオカミではなく？

Not from a tiger? トラではなく？

Not from a shark? サメではなく？

We hunt animals. 私たちは動物を狩ります。

We eat animals. 動物を食べます。

We have always done this. 昔からずっとそうしてきました。

But, / something stops us. でも、/ 何かが私たちを止めます。

Something specific. 何か具体的なものが。

Something / we never really questioned. 私たちが / ほとんど疑問に思ったことすらないものです。

And the answer / is not what you think. そしてその答えは / あなたが思っているものではありません。

Most people assume / it is just a cultural thing. 多くの人は / 単なる文化的なものだと思っています。

A taboo. タブーだ、と。

Uh, / we don't do that. 「ええと、/ そういうことはしない。」

But, / that is only the surface. でも、/ それは表面的な話にすぎません。

Underneath, / there is / a biological reason. その奥には、/ 生物学的な理由があります。

A chemical reason. 化学的な理由もあります。

And a reason / buried so deep / in your evolutionary memory そして、/ 進化の記憶の中に / あまりにも深く埋め込まれた理由があり、

that your body / already knew the answer あなたの身体は / すでにその答えを知っていたのです。

before science / confirmed it. 科学が / それを確認するよりも前に。

Stay with this / all the way to the end 最後まで / ここについてきてください。

because / the last part / is not really about predators / at all. なぜなら、/ 最後の部分は / 実は捕食者についての話では / 全くないからです。

It is about you それはあなた自身、

and what / you are made of. そしてあなたが / 何でできているのかについての話です。

Let us start / with the most obvious question. まずは / 最も分かりやすい質問から始めましょう。

Could you even eat / a lion / if you wanted to? そもそも、食べようと思えば / ライオンを / 食べられるのでしょうか？

The answer is yes. 答えは「はい」です。

Technically, / the meat is not poisonous 厳密に言えば、/ その肉は毒ではありません。

in the way / that a mushroom might be. キノコの中には毒を持つものがありますが、/ そういう意味での毒ではないのです。

You would not / drop dead / from a single bite. 一口食べただけで / その場で死ぬ / ということはありません。

Some people / have eaten lion. 実際に / ライオンを食べた人もいます。

Some indigenous hunters / in history / have eaten wolf. 歴史上、一部の先住民の狩猟民は / オオカミを / 食べてきました。

There are accounts / of people cooking / bear, alligator, shark. クマ、ワニ、サメを / 調理して食べたという記録もあります。

So, / the meat exists. つまり、/ 肉そのものは存在します。

The option exists. 食べるという選択肢も存在します。

But, / there is a reason / that almost no culture on Earth しかし、/ 地球上のほとんどすべての文化が、

built a tradition / around eating predators. 捕食者を食べる伝統を / 築いてこなかった理由があります。

And that reason / is one of the most elegant laws / in all of nature. そしてその理由は / 自然界に存在する最も洗練された法則の一つです。

It is called / the 10% rule. それは / 「10％ルール」と呼ばれています。

Here is / what it means. どういう意味かというと、

Every time / energy moves up / the food chain, エネルギーが / 食物連鎖を / 一段上へ移動するたびに、

90% of it / disappears. その90％が / 失われます。

A blade of grass / holds energy / from the sun. 一本の草は / 太陽から得た / エネルギーを蓄えています。

A deer / eats the grass. シカが / その草を食べます。

But, / the deer / only keeps / about 10% of that energy. しかし、/ シカが保持できるのは / そのエネルギーの / わずか約10％です。

The rest / is burned, / lost as heat, 残りは / 消費されたり、/ 熱として失われたり、

used to keep / the deer / moving and breathing. シカが / 動いたり呼吸したりするために / 使われます。

Now, / a lion / eats the deer. そして今度は、/ ライオンが / そのシカを食べます。

Same thing / happens. 同じことが / 起こります。

The lion / only keeps / 10% of the deer's energy. ライオンが保持できるのは / シカのエネルギーの / わずか10％です。

So, / by the time / you are sitting / at the top of the food chain, つまり、/ 食物連鎖の頂点に / あなたがいる頃には、

you are feeding on / the leftovers / of leftovers. 食べているのは / 残り物の / さらに残り物なのです。

Think about / what that means / in practice. これが / 実際にはどういう意味なのか / 考えてみてください。

A lion / needs to eat / around 15 to 20 lb of meat ライオンは / 15～20ポンド（約7～9kg）の肉を / 食べる必要があります。

just to survive / a single day. たった一日 / 生き延びるためだけに。

That is / an enormous cost, これは / 莫大なコストです。

an enormous amount / of prey 膨大な量の獲物を

burned down / to just enough / to keep one lion alive. 一頭のライオンを生かすのに必要な量まで / 消費してしまうのです。

Now, / imagine / you are a prehistoric human. では、/ あなたが / 先史時代の人間だったと想像してください。

You are hungry. あなたは空腹です。

You have a choice. 選択肢があります。

Do you risk your life / fighting a lion 命を危険にさらして / ライオンと戦い、

for 15 lb / of low-energy meat, 15ポンドの / エネルギー効率の低い肉を手に入れるのか、

or do you hunt / the deer, それとも / シカを狩るのか。

which costs you / far less danger シカなら / はるかに危険が少なく、

and gives you / far more bang / for your calorie? しかも摂取カロリーに対して / はるかに大きな見返りが得られます。

The math / was obvious. 計算すれば / 明らかでした。

The answer / was always obvious. 答えは / いつだって明白だったのです。

You go for / the prey, / not the predator. 狙うのは / 捕食者ではなく、/ 獲物です。

But, / here is where / it gets stranger, しかし、/ ここから / さらに奇妙な話になります。

because / the energy problem / is only part of the story. なぜなら、/ エネルギーの問題は / 話の一部にすぎないからです。

The other part / is invisible, もう一つの部分は / 目に見えません。

and it is sitting / inside every apex predator / alive today. そしてそれは / 現在生きているすべての頂点捕食者の / 体内に存在します。

It is called / biomagnification. それは / 「生物濃縮」と呼ばれます。

Here is / how it works. その仕組みは / こうです。

Toxins / enter the food chain / at the bottom, 毒素が / 食物連鎖の底辺から / 入ってきます。

pollutants in water, 水中の汚染物質、

heavy metals in soil, 土壌中の重金属、

chemicals / absorbed by plants / and tiny creatures. 植物や小さな生物に / 吸収される化学物質です。

A small fish / eats / thousands of tiny organisms. 小さな魚が / 何千もの / 微小な生物を食べます。

It absorbs / a small dose of toxin / each time, そのたびに / 少量の毒素を / 吸収します。

not enough / to kill it, 死ぬほどではありません。

but it builds up. しかし、それが / 蓄積していきます。

A bigger fish / eats / thousands of small fish. より大きな魚が / 何千匹もの小魚を / 食べます。

Now, / the toxin / is concentrated, すると、/ 毒素が / 濃縮され、

higher dose. より高い濃度になります。

Still survivable. それでもまだ / 生存できる程度です。

Then, / a predator fish / eats / thousands of medium fish. そして、/ 捕食魚が / 何千匹もの中型魚を / 食べます。

The toxin / concentrates again. 毒素は / さらに濃縮されます。

By the time / you reach the top, そして / 頂点にたどり着く頃には、

the concentration / is not just / slightly higher. その濃度は / 単に少し高くなる / というレベルではありません。

It can be / millions of times higher 何百万倍も高くなる / ことさえあります。

than it was / at the bottom. 食物連鎖の底辺に / 存在していたときよりも。

This is not / a theory. これは / 理論上の話ではありません。

This is / measurable. 実際に / 測定できます。

This is / documented. 記録・実証されています。

In 1966, 1966年、

a scientist / named Charles Wurster チャールズ・ワースターという / 科学者が、

studied / eagles / dying along the Great Lakes. 五大湖周辺で / 死んでいくワシを / 調査しました。

The eagles were not being

poisoned directly. They were eating fish, but those fish had absorbed DDT, a

chemical pesticide, through the whole chain below them. The DDT had

concentrated so much by the top that the eagles' eggs were cracking before they

could hatch. The eagles were dying not from what they ate, but from everything

their food had eaten before them. This is what sits inside every predator,

mercury, lead, PCBs, synthetic chemicals that the body cannot break down. They

accumulate in fat tissue. They build up over a lifetime. You have already felt

the edge of this yourself. You have been told to eat tuna carefully. Not because

tuna is dangerous, but because tuna is a large predatory fish. And large

predatory fish sit high enough on the chain that their mercury levels are

measurable, significant, worth monitoring. Now, imagine eating a

creature that sat even higher, a land apex predator, a bear, a wolf, an orca.

The load inside those animals is not just higher, it is often extraordinary.

And that is before we even get to the parasites. Because predators carry

something else inside them, something your immune system would not enjoy at

all. Predators eat raw flesh frequently. Their digestive systems and muscle

tissue host parasites that have evolved alongside that lifestyle for millions of

years. The most famous is a tiny organism called Trichinella. It lives in

the muscle tissue of carnivores. When a human eats infected meat without cooking

it to the right temperature, the larvae hatch inside the intestine. They migrate

into your muscles. Your body swells. Your fever climbs. The pain is severe.

Historical cases were often fatal. Bear meat is one of the most documented

sources of Trichinella in the world, and bears are the one large predator that

humans have occasionally eaten throughout history. Every culture that

ate bear knew you had to cook it thoroughly. Not because it tasted

better, because if you didn't, the consequences were brutal. Now, think

about a true apex predator, an animal that has spent decades accumulating

parasites, toxins, and biological baggage from everything it ever

consumed. Your body is not equipped for that. It was never meant to be. But here

is the part that makes this even more fascinating. Because there is a force

even older than the chemistry, something that evolved inside you long before you

understood any of this. It is disgust. Researchers studying human food

psychology have found something remarkable. Humans have an instinctive

aversion to the idea of eating carnivores. It is not just cultural. It

appears in children before they have been taught it. It appears across

cultures with no contact with each other. Paul Rozin, a psychologist at the

University of Pennsylvania, spent decades studying the disgust response.

He found that humans categorize meat from carnivores as deeply aversive at an

almost pre-rational level. The explanation is evolutionary. Your

ancient ancestors who felt nothing about eating a predator sometimes died from

toxins, from parasites, from the chemical load carried inside apex

carnivores. But the ancestors who felt a gut-level resistance, who found the idea

unappetizing without knowing why, those ancestors lived. They survived. They

passed that feeling down. You inherited it. Right now, if I ask you to imagine

eating a juicy wolf steak, something in you would react before your brain

finished the thought. That is not culture. That is millions of years of

your ancestors surviving exactly the situations that killed the others. Your

disgust is your ancestral memory speaking. But now, here is the twist,

because not every predator is off-limits, and the exceptions reveal

something important. Humans do eat predatory fish with caution. We eat

alligator. We eat bear. We eat shark in some cultures. We eat squid and octopus,

which are ferocious hunters of the sea. What these have in common is that they

sit just low enough on the chain or are consumed just rarely enough that the

load stays manageable. The rule is not that you can never eat a predator. The

rule is that the higher you go, the more dangerous the accumulation becomes. And

the animals at the very top, the lions, the wolves, the orcas, the tigers, they

have spent entire lifetimes concentrating what the whole world below

them contained. They are not just animals. They are living archives of

everything the food chain has touched, and eating them would mean consuming all

of that, compressed, stored, waiting. Your body knew this. Your ancestors

learned it the hard way so you wouldn't have to. Now, here is the part that will

change how you look at your next meal. Every animal you have ever eaten, the

chicken, the beef, the fish, every single one of them sat low enough on on

chain that the chemical load was survivable. Low enough that the parasite

risk was manageable. Low enough that your ancient disgust response didn't

fire. The food you eat every single day is the result of millions of years of

selection pressure. You didn't choose it randomly. Your entire culinary tradition

was shaped by survival logic so old it predates language. The cow on your plate

is not just food. It is the result of a biological calculation your ancestors

ran for generations without ever writing it down. And the animals at the top of

the chain, the ones you would never consider eating, they are there because

that is where danger lives at every level, in every form. Predators are not

just dangerous to hunt, they are dangerous to consume. The whole system

was built to keep you away from them. And somehow, without chemistry

textbooks, without microscopes, without knowing what a toxin was or what a

parasite did, your ancestors already knew. They passed the knowledge down the

only way evolution allows. They made it feel wrong. And you are here today

because it worked.
